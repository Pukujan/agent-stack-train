## Why this exists

Start with a situation the reader recognizes. Show one concrete example of what the person is trying to do, where the current path breaks, and what consequence follows. Explain why this project deserves to exist. Ground the example in repository evidence or label it as hypothetical.

**Use one short bold anchor when it helps a skimming reader retain the point.** Keep the paragraph readable without the styling.

## What this project is

Say who this is for, what the project helps them do, and what the project is not. Keep the promise concrete and bounded.

## What you can make or use

Show the useful outputs, workflows, examples, or decisions a reader can reach. Use a short list or table only after the story is clear.

## How it works

Explain the mechanism in reader-sized steps. Introduce technical terms at the moment they help; move deep architecture, commands, and schemas below the human explanation.

![Supporting visual for one mechanism or boundary](path/to/supporting-visual.png)

## Evidence and boundaries

Link important **product** claims directly to code, tests, data, committed artifacts, experiments, or external sources. Label work as shipped, experimentally supported, planned, or unknown. State what the cited evidence supports, what it leaves unproven, and what the project does not claim. Pin repository citations to the inspected commit and useful path/line or heading; use direct external links. Do not cite CGM to justify the product story.

Give the smallest useful path to a first result, then link to the technical setup and full documentation. Add a separate build/reproduce path when developers or researchers need different steps from the primary reader.
