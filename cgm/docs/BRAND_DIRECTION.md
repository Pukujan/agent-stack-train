# Story Loop brand direction

This is the visual and verbal direction for Content Generation Modules. The repository keeps its descriptive product name; “Story Loop” is the internal brand idea that gives the system a memorable shape.

## The brand idea

**Give every project a clearer story.** A repository can contain strong evidence while its public story remains scattered. The helper gives that evidence a loop: research first, write for humans, make the visual explain the idea, and review before the result is treated as finished.

The loop is about attention, connection, and accountability. It should feel like a working editorial system with a human relationship at its center, not a decorative automation theme.

## Audience and personality

The audience includes maintainers, agents, designers, researchers, product people, and engineers who need to turn project material into something another human can understand and use.

The personality is:

- **Approachable:** invite the reader into the work before explaining the machinery.
- **Curious:** show the questions, evidence, and decisions behind the output.
- **Bright:** use color and movement to make the system feel alive and easy to enter.
- **Organized:** give every artifact a role, a path, and a review decision.
- **Honest:** distinguish what is shipped, supported by an experiment, planned, or unknown.

Avoid generic enterprise AI, dark cyber imagery, sterile dashboards, exaggerated promises, and playful details that are unrelated to the content problem.

## Reference analysis

The strongest visual references are the repository’s own prior reviewed outputs: [Harness on Steroids](https://github.com/Pukujan/harness-on-steroids) and [Eval Lab](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab). The [Peeps case study](https://www.linamakesnoise.com/peeps) remains useful as a product-design reference for audience, personality, onboarding, and flow, but it is not an asset to copy.

The prior repository work establishes the visual signals this helper should now inherit:

- [Harness’s hero](https://github.com/Pukujan/harness-on-steroids/blob/main/docs/content-system-assets/hero.png) uses an anime-inspired human and friendly robot to make “look, act, verify” feel like a relationship and a loop.
- [Eval Lab’s banner](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/blob/main/assets/eval-lab-banner.png) pairs a clear promise with a human, a companion, and a simple evidence panel.
- [Eval Lab’s problem image](https://github.com/Pukujan/Eval-lab/blob/main/assets/eval-lab-problem.png) turns the product tension into a visible scene instead of a technical paragraph.
- Their square and portrait assets keep the same characters, palette, and lighting while giving each image a different explanatory job.

This repository carries forward the method—audience, personality, metaphor, flow, and human relationship—while changing the subject to repository evidence and content UX. It uses an original content designer and a small story-guide companion, with a research-to-review loop and readable content artifacts. The characters and compositions are new; the continuity principle comes from the prior work.

## Visual system

Use this palette as a starting point for generated visuals and HTML demos:

| Token | Color | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night ink | `#111B4D` | background, headings, high-contrast type |
| Violet | `#8F7CFF` | section emphasis, imagination, variation |
| Cyan | `#63D9FF` | evidence, navigation, connection lines |
| Coral | `#FF8A70` | action, warmth, human energy |
| Warm cream | `#F4F1FF` | readable title copy and quiet panels |
| Mint | `#94E3CB` | confirmation, continuity, calm |
| Gold | `#FFD28A` | evidence highlights and small moments of optimism |

Pair a bold geometric sans or soft display face with a clear humanist sans for body copy. Use anime-inspired character illustration, rounded content cards, glass or paper panels, soft neon connector lines, and warm desk light against a blue-violet evening setting. Let the characters and one simple diagram do the explaining; do not use visual noise to manufacture complexity.

## Image direction

Every image should answer one reader question. The hero introduces the movement from scattered evidence to usable content. A problem image shows why a full repository can still leave a reader guessing. A supporting image explains the loop between research, writing, visuals, implementation, and review. Keep the designer and companion visible so the work feels cared for and accountable.

For narrative raster assets, use one exact short title and one exact subtitle in quiet space. Keep the subject, content page, and transformation visible on narrow crops. Avoid fake metrics, dense fake interface labels, unreadable copy, and generic “AI” imagery.

The committed examples are:

- `assets/marketing/hero-story-loop.png` — wide story introduction;
- `assets/marketing/problem-scattered-story.png` — wide problem explanation;
- `assets/marketing/story-loop-square.png` — square workflow explanation.

Their prompts, exact copy, alt text, crop rules, and review decisions are recorded in `assets/marketing/IMAGE_NOTES.md` and `assets/marketing/asset-manifest.json`.

## Voice and emphasis

Write like a generous product designer who respects the reader’s time. Lead with a concrete situation, use active verbs, explain technical terms when they become useful, and bold only the phrase that a skimming reader should retain. The brand should feel warm because it is clear, not because it makes unsupported promises.
